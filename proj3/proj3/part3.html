Archlab ChampSim Project 3

Part 3: Paper Reading, Prefetcher Design, Implementation, and Evaluation

In this part, you will first read a state-of-the-art research paper on hardware prefetching and analyze its design principles. Based on the paper and your understanding of memory access prediction, you will then design and implement your own hardware prefetcher in ChampSim. Finally, you will evaluate your prefetcher performance and participate in a competitive benchmark ranking.

Task 1: Paper Reading and Summary

Reference Paper

Cui, Y., Chen, W., Cheng, X., & Yi, J. (2024). Hyperion: A Highly Effective Page and PC Based Delta Prefetcher. ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (TACO).

Download PDF from ACM DL

You are required to read the paper above and write a concise summary. The goal of this task is to understand the design principles of modern hardware prefetchers and use them as inspiration for your own prefetcher design.

Your summary should cover:

  1. Background: What challenges do modern processors face when predicting future memory accesses? What limitations exist in traditional spatial, stride, or delta-based prefetchers?

  2. Core Design & Mechanisms: How does Hyperion leverage Page information and Instruction Pointer (PC) information to track memory access patterns? Explain its key tables, metadata structures, delta tracking mechanisms, and prefetch generation strategy.

  3. Design Insights: Identify the key ideas from Hyperion that can be applied to designing a new prefetcher. Discuss possible improvements, extensions, or alternative designs.

(Note: This is a custom prefetcher design task; reproducing Hyperion is not required. The purpose of this task is to understand the paper and derive your own prefetcher design.) Of course, you may also choose to reproduce Hyperion and improve it on that basis.

Task 2: Designing and Implementing Your Own Prefetcher in ChampSim

ChampSim exposes cache events through C++ prefetcher module callbacks.

In this task, you will design and implement your own prefetcher based on the ideas learned from the reference paper. Your design should include a prediction mechanism, metadata tracking structures, and a prefetch generation strategy.

Your prefetcher may be inspired by Hyperion or other existing prefetching techniques, such as:

  • PC-based stride prefetching
  • Delta correlation prefetching
  • Page-based spatial prefetching
  • Region-based prefetching
  • Hybrid prefetching combining multiple predictors
  • Any novel prefetching strategy designed by yourself

Course ChampSim API

  • prefetcher_cache_operate() receives typed addresses, the PC, hit status, useful_prefetch, access type, and metadata. In the provided header, cache_hit is uint8_t; zero means miss and nonzero means hit. Return metadata_in when your design does not modify metadata.
  • Activation: the cache calls your callback only for access types in its prefetch_activate mask, and never for requests generated by the cache's own prefetcher. In the shared platform, L1D calls it on LOAD and PREFETCH, not on WRITE or RFO tag checks. Keep these platform settings unchanged.
  • addr and ip are champsim::address, not integer block indices. In the shared L1D baseline, addr is a physical data address; ip identifies the initiating instruction. Data addresses may contain byte-offset bits, so normalize them consistently for history and fill lookups. A request without an initiating demand PC can have a zero ip.
  • useful_prefetch is true only when an access hits a line brought in by a prefetch. The simulator's USEFUL counter also counts demand requests that merge with an in-flight prefetch, so the two are not identical; see Part 2.
  • type is the scoped access_type enum. Compare it with values such as access_type::LOAD, access_type::RFO, or access_type::PREFETCH.
  • prefetcher_cache_fill() reports the fill address, long set/way indices, a uint8_t prefetch flag, evicted address, and metadata. A nonzero prefetch flag identifies a prefetch fill as reported by the simulator. A bypass has way == intern_->NUM_WAY and a default-constructed evicted address; do not index per-way state for a bypass. Return the metadata to associate with the filled line.
  • intern_ points to the attached cache. Include cache.h in the implementation when accessing fields or methods such as intern_->NUM_WAY or intern_->get_mshr_occupancy_ratio().
  • prefetch_line(pf_addr, fill_this_level, metadata) takes a typed byte address and a boolean fill choice. true fills the attached cache (L1D here); false forwards the request through the lower hierarchy without filling L1D. The boolean is not a cache-level number. The return value only says the request entered the prefetch interface's queue; it does not guarantee a memory transaction or a later fill callback. Handle rejection when maintaining request state or implementing retries.
  • Optional callbacks: prefetcher_initialize() runs once during initialization; prefetcher_cycle_operate() runs once per attached-cache operate tick, after its main tag/fill work; prefetcher_final_stats() can print custom statistics. Declare and define any optional callback you use. The branch callback belongs to instruction-prefetcher use and is not needed for this L1D task.

Use the typed address helpers instead of passing a shifted integer as a byte address:

const champsim::block_number block{addr};
const champsim::page_number page{addr};
const uint64_t block_key = block.to<uint64_t>();
const uint64_t page_key = page.to<uint64_t>();
const uint64_t block_in_page =
    champsim::page_offset{addr}.to<uint64_t>() / BLOCK_SIZE;
const champsim::address line_addr{block};
const champsim::address next_line_addr{block + 1};

block_key and page_key are integer indices suitable for table keys; line_addr and next_line_addr are typed byte addresses suitable for prefetch_line(). Apply the same block normalization in operate and fill callbacks.

Timestamps and Predictor State

Use intern_->current_time as your only clock source. It is available in both operate and fill callbacks, shares the cache's timebase, and needs no extra state. Record it on every event and convert elapsed time to local cache cycles with intern_->clock_period:

const auto request_time = intern_->current_time;
// Store request_time in a module-owned table; retrieve it on the later event.
const auto elapsed_cycles =
    (intern_->current_time - request_time) / intern_->clock_period;

Do not use access counts, host wall-clock time, or a second timestamp source. Keep predictor tables and timing state continuous across the warmup/measurement boundary; ChampSim resets its native measurement counters itself. Track rejected requests, repeated accesses, fills, and stale entries consistently.

Your prefetcher implementation will be evaluated using a suite of ChampSim trace workloads (including both prefetch-sensitive and undisclosed hidden benchmarks).

All baseline and custom prefetchers must run on the shared evaluation platform defined in Part 2, with 20M warmup instructions followed by 100M simulation instructions per trace. Attach your prefetcher only to L1D. Do not use your optimized Part 1 cache configuration. The same fixed platform and instruction window apply to hidden benchmarks and leaderboard runs.

Extract the counters and calculate the metrics yourself using the definitions, formulas, and zero-denominator rules in Part 2. Submit raw logs, counter CSV files, and calculated results for baseline and custom prefetchers.

Reuse Part 2 Baselines

You may reuse the six Part 2 baseline results (no, next_line, and ip_stride on both workloads) when they use the same simulator revision, baseline implementations, CPU/cache/memory configuration, trace files and starting positions, and 20M warmup + 100M measurement window. Relative Ranking

The relative ranking is a separate grading item from the local lab3-auto-run.py score. It evaluates your prefetcher against next_line on the evaluation traces, including hidden benchmarks.

Students are ranked by the geometric mean IPC speedup relative to next_line:

G=(i=1∏N​IPCnext_line,i​IPCcustom,i​​)1/N.

Each trace has equal weight. Run next_line and your custom prefetcher on the same platform and instruction window for each trace. This ranking metric uses next_line as its reference; the general-purpose Speedup column defined in Part 2 uses no. Report the per-trace ratios to next_line and their geometric mean separately.

Sort by evaluation score from highest to lowest and divide rank positions into 20 approximately equal-sized bands. The highest band earns 2.0 points, and each subsequent band earns 0.1 points less, down to 0.1 points for the lowest band. Tied scores share the same rank and points, using competition ranking (e.g., 1, 2, 2, 4).

You can self-test your implementation on the online evaluation platform used in Project 2 to see where you currently stand. The official evaluation is run centrally for all submissions after the deadline, and the ranking uses that result. The online evaluation and the local 16-trace suite share only two traces, 603.bwaves_s-1080B.champsimtrace.xz and 654.roms_s-1021B.champsimtrace.xz; the other evaluation traces differ, so treat your local score as a rough self-check rather than a prediction of your rank.

Grading Structure

Part 3 contributes 11 points: 5 points for the paper summary and 6 points for design, implementation, and performance. The 6 points count toward the 18-point experimental component of the project.

  1. Paper Summary (5 pts): Background and motivation: 1 pt; core architecture and prediction mechanisms: 3 pts; design insights: 1 pt.

  2. Prefetcher Design & Implementation (2 pts): Students should provide:

    • Description of their prefetcher design.
    • Motivation behind the design choices.
    • Explanation of prediction mechanisms and metadata structures.
    • Correct implementation in ChampSim.
    • Analysis of IPC, accuracy, coverage estimate, and speedup.

  3. Performance Evaluation (4 pts):

    • Absolute Performance (2 pts): Obtain score >= 100 with the local lab3-auto-run.py to earn the full 2 points.

    • Relative Ranking (up to 2 pts): Ranked by the geometric mean IPC ratio relative to next_line. See Relative Ranking for the metric, bands and evaluation details.
