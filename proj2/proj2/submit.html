Archlab gem5 Project 2

Submission

Submission Instructions

  • Please submit the deliverables for each part in order, clearly structured in a report form (PDF format). You NEED to submit your report, source codes, custom trace file, technical documentation, and paper summary.
  • Pack all your submission files into a single archive (.zip or .tar.gz) and submit it as an attachment at course.pku.edu.cn. The title of the attachment and archive should follow the format: Student-ID_Name_Proj2 (e.g., 123456789_WangXiaoming_Proj2).
  • You can also send the archive to 2501112042@stu.pku.edu.cn. The titles of your email AND attachment must both be Student-ID_Name_Proj2.
  • DO NOT PLAGIARIZE. We will randomly select 10 students for an oral defense and Q&A session regarding their implementations and trace designs.

Late Policy

  • You are allocated 3 slip days in total (shared across all course projects), which can be used to extend project deadlines (e.g., 1 project extended by 3 days, or 3 projects each extended by 1 day).
  • All projects are strictly due at 23:59:59 on the deadline date. No exceptions.
  • Late submissions without slip days will incur a 20% penalty per day (1 second late = 1 hour late = 1 day late).

Deliverables

Your submitted package must contain the following components:

  1. Lab Report (Report.pdf):
    • Part 1 & 2 Evaluation: Figures and tables showing Branch Prediction Accuracy (and MPKI) and CPU IPC for built-in predictors (bimodal, gshare, hashed_perceptron) and your simple_bp on shell_sort and spfa traces.
    • Part 3 Paper Summary: A 1–2 page summary of the Jiménez & Lin paper covering motivations, key challenges, and perceptron mechanisms.
    • Part 3 Technical Documentation: Detailed explanation of your custom your_perceptron architecture, parameter selection (s.t. k,θ, table sizes), and any optimization techniques used.
    • Part 4 Hard Trace Analysis: Detailed description of your custom 10M instruction workload design, the control-flow tricks used, and a performance comparison table of baseline BPs running on your generated trace.
  2. Source Codes:
    • branch/simple_bp/: Code files (simple_bp.h and simple_bp.cc).
    • branch/your_perceptron/: Code files (your_perceptron.h and your_perceptron.cc).
    • hard_workload.cpp (or .c): C/C++ source code used to generate your Part 4 hard trace.
  3. Hard Trace File:
    • <student_id>_hard.champsimtrace.xz: Your compressed 10M instruction trace file for Part 4.

Grading Scheme (20 Points Total)

Your final score for Project 2 will be calculated based on the following breakdown:

  • Part 1 & 2: Baseline & Simple BP Evaluation (3 pts)
    • 3 pts: Reasonable performance statistics (Accuracy/MPKI, IPC) provided for bimodal, gshare, hashed_perceptron, and simple_bp on standard workloads.
  • Part 3: Perceptron Paper Summary (4 pts)
    • 4 pts: Comprehensive summary covering motivations, challenges, and architectural designs (Lack of any key section: -1 pt each).
  • Part 3: Custom BP Implementation & Performance Ranking (8 pts)
    • 3 pts: Compilable and functionally correct your_perceptron code that passes simulation runs without errors.
    • 2 pts: Performance surpassing simple_bp and comparable to bimodal / gshare.
    • 3 pts: Competitive performance matching/exceeding hashed_perceptron, supported by clear technical explanations and high leaderboard ranking.
  • Part 4: Hard Trace Benchmark & Stress Test (5 pts)
    • 2 pts: Valid C/C++ source code and properly formatted 10M-instruction trace file (.champsimtrace.xz) that runs without crashing.
    • 3 pts: Trace hardness evaluation score based on MPKI on baseline BPs (Harder traces with higher MPKI achieve higher scores; Top 10% hardest traces receive full score + ranking bonus).
